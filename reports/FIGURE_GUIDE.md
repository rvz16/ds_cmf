# Figure guide and concrete interpretations

All figures are generated by `python scripts/train.py` from the supplied data. Display clipping is used only where stated; model inputs are not clipped by EDA plotting code.

## `reports/figures/price_and_spread.png`

The mid-price falls from roughly 0.0110 at the start toward 0.0078 at the end, with its lowest region around 5 August. Spread spikes cluster around that volatile drawdown; the 99th percentile is 1.998 bps versus a 0.117 bps median.

## `reports/figures/distributions.png`

One-second returns are sharply concentrated at zero but heavy-tailed: the 1st and 99th percentiles are approximately -10.50 and +10.68 bps, while observed extremes exceed ±160 bps. The spread is usually tight but has a long right tail, so a constant-spread assumption would be inappropriate.

## `reports/figures/depth_and_imbalance.png`

Bid and ask ten-level depth distributions overlap substantially, while depth imbalance spans both signs. Per-second traded volume is broad and right-skewed even after `log1p`, motivating scale-stabilized depth/volume features rather than raw amounts.

## `reports/figures/autocorrelation.png`

Lag-1 raw-return autocorrelation is only 0.0378, but lag-1 absolute-return autocorrelation is 0.3336 and decays more slowly. Directional linear dependence is weak, while volatility clustering is economically material.

## `reports/figures/correlations.png`

Microprice displacement and top-level imbalance are nearly redundant (Spearman 0.939). Contemporaneous return is strongly associated with OFI (0.788) and trade imbalance (0.509); spread also co-moves with rolling volatility (0.620). These are descriptive contemporaneous dependencies, not out-of-sample predictive claims.

## `reports/figures/intraday_patterns.png`

Activity and volatility vary by UTC hour: hour 16 has the highest mean absolute return (3.19 bps) and trade count (67.2 per second), while hour 23 is quieter (24.8 trades per second). With only six days and an unknown venue, this is a sample pattern rather than a stable seasonality claim.

## `reports/figures/target_balance.png`

The ±3 bps neutral class accounts for 76.8% of 1-second labels, 46.2% at 5 seconds, and 33.9% at 10 seconds. This explains why accuracy overstates 1-second performance and why macro-F1 is the primary predictive metric.

## `reports/figures/confusion_matrix.png`

For the 5-second LightGBM, neutral recall is 0.750, versus 0.139 for down and 0.264 for up. The model remains biased toward the neutral class despite nonlinear features, which limits directional trading usefulness.

## `reports/figures/test_equity_curves.png`

Under realistic-base costs, every active 5-second strategy finishes below the no-trade majority baseline. The largely monotone declines show that turnover costs dominate intermittent gross gains rather than losses being caused by one isolated event.
